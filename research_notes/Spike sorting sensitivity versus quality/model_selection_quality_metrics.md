## Q1. Do model-based (probabilistic/Bayesian) spike sorters give a principled criterion for including small spikes and small units?

### Takeaway
Many model-based sorters derive the *form* of the detection and assignment rule from a likelihood or posterior. In the cleanest cases, detection is a log-likelihood-ratio test and the threshold is set by prior log odds (the firing probability). None of them removes the free choice. The decision moves into priors or hyperparameters: the Bernoulli spike prior, the Dirichlet-process concentration α, the sparsity penalty λ, the significance level of a unimodality test, or a BIC/evidence penalty. Each of these rests on a noise model (Gaussian or t, stationary, whitened) that is wrong for dense-probe data, because background activity is mostly other neurons' spikes. Kilosort, including KS4, is a template-matching or deconvolution method with *ad hoc* thresholds (Th_universal=9, Th_learned=8). It has no stated likelihood-derived criterion.

### Cited Findings
- **Lewicki 1994** (Neural Comput 6:1005): Bayesian Gaussian-mixture modelling of spike waveforms. The number of classes is chosen by the Bayesian evidence (marginal likelihood), and overlapping spikes are decomposed probabilistically. **Lewicki 1998** (Network 9:R53) reviews this and states the general view that sorting is model-based inference with explicit noise assumptions. — [Lewicki 1994, DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.1994.6.5.1005); [Lewicki 1998, DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1088/0954-898X_9_4_001)
- **Shoham, Fellows & Normann 2003** (J Neurosci Methods 127:111): a multivariate t-distribution mixture fitted by EM. Heavy tails make it robust to outliers and overlaps. The number of components is chosen by a penalized-likelihood (model-order) criterion; verify whether it is BIC or MDL. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0165-0270(03)00120-1)
- **Wood & Black 2008** (J Neurosci Methods 173:1–12): an infinite Gaussian mixture (Dirichlet process mixture). It does not produce one best spike train. Quote: "we estimate a probabilistic model of spike trains given the observed data", which aims "to represent this uncertainty explicitly and account for its effect on spike train analysis." Cluster count is a posterior quantity. Prior beliefs about cluster weights are encoded in the Dirichlet hyperparameters (α/K in the finite-K limit). — [Wood & Black 2008 preprint, fetched via search](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~fwood/papers/Wood-JNM-2008.pdf); [Wood & Black EMBS 2006, fetched via search](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~fwood/papers/Wood-EMBS-2006.pdf)
- **Pillow, Shlens, Chichilnisky & Simoncelli 2013** (PLoS ONE 8:e62123): voltage is modelled as "a linear combination of spike waveforms plus a stochastic background component of correlated Gaussian noise". A Bernoulli prior over binary spike trains gives a posterior, which a greedy "binary pursuit" algorithm maximizes. The authors also "develop diagnostic tools that can be used to assess errors in spike sorting in the absence of ground truth." — [PLoS ONE via RePEc, fetched via search](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0062123.html); [code](https://github.com/pillowlab/BinaryPursuitSpikeSorting)
- **Franke, Quian Quiroga, Hierlemann & Obermayer 2015** (J Comput Neurosci), "Bayes optimal template matching for spike sorting – combining Fisher discriminant analysis with optimal filtering": gives "a Bayesian interpretation" of matched-filter outputs "with a spike detection threshold computed analytically". It is implemented as a linear filter bank derived from the templates and resolves overlapping spikes with high accuracy. This is the closest published analogue of a principled threshold for Kilosort-style template matching. — [PMC4420847, fetched via search](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4420847/); companion in-vivo overlap paper (Franke, Pröpper, Alle, Meier, Geiger, Obermayer, Munk 2015) — [PMC4725215](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4725215)
- **Franke et al. 2010** (J Comput Neurosci 29:127): an earlier online filter-based detection and classification algorithm that resolves overlaps instantly. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10827-009-0163-5)
- **Ekanadham, Tranchina & Simoncelli 2014** (J Neurosci Methods), continuous basis pursuit: spikes are recovered by L1-regularized (sparse) inference over continuous time shifts and amplitudes. The sparsity weight corresponds to a prior on spike rate, so it is still a tuning parameter. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2013.10.001)
- **Calabrese & Paninski 2011** (J Neurosci Methods): a Kalman-filter mixture model in which cluster means drift over time, fitted by EM. It extends the likelihood framework to nonstationarity. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2010.12.002)
- **Carlson et al. 2014** (IEEE TBME): Bayesian nonparametric joint dictionary learning plus a mixture model for multichannel sorting, with an inferred number of units. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1109/TBME.2013.2275751)
- **ISO-SPLIT / MountainSort** (Magland & Barnett 2015 arXiv; Chung et al. 2017 Neuron 95:1381): clusters are split or merged using a nonparametric unimodality test based on isotonic regression, with no Gaussian assumption. The number of clusters follows from repeated hypothesis tests at a fixed threshold, so the test's significance level is the free parameter. — [ISO-SPLIT arXiv, not fetched](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04841); [Chung et al. 2017, DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.08.030)
- **YASS** (Lee et al. NeurIPS 2017; bioRxiv 2020): neural-network detection, outlier "triage", then mixture-model clustering and deconvolution. Triage deliberately discards hard spikes, trading false negatives for purity. — [bioRxiv 2020, DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1101/2020.03.18.997924)
- **Kilosort4** (Pachitariu, Sridhar, Pennington & Stringer 2024, Nat Methods 21:914): template deconvolution (matching pursuit) with iterative detect-and-subtract. Universal templates are unit-norm, and their variance explained is the squared dot product with the data. The model is called generative or reconstructive, but the text gives no explicit full-model likelihood. Clustering is graph-based: K-means++ initialization with 200 clusters, modularity reassignment (γ=1), and a merge tree built by lowering γ. A split is always made if the pair's cross-correlogram is refractory; otherwise it is made only if the regression-axis projection is bimodal. Global merges are considered for pairs with waveform correlation > 0.5 and are made if the CCG is refractory. The visible text gives no principled criterion for the thresholds. — [KS4 bioRxiv preprint, fetched](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.07.523036v1.full); [Nat Methods record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02232-7)
- In this repo, `Th_universal` (default 9) and `Th_learned` (default 8) are documented only as "Spike detection threshold for universal/learned templates", which correspond to Th(1) and Th(2) in earlier versions. — [local code: kilosort/parameters.py](file:///C:/code/github/Kilosort/kilosort/parameters.py)
- Original Kilosort (Pachitariu et al. 2016) framed template matching as fitting a generative model with per-spike amplitudes. — [bioRxiv 061481, DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1101/061481)

### Inferences
- If whitened noise is unit-variance Gaussian, adding a spike of a unit-norm template at its best-fitting amplitude A raises the log-likelihood by about A²/2. A Bayes-optimal (MAP) rule in the style of Pillow and Franke would add the spike when A²/2 exceeds the log prior odds log((1−p)/p), where p is the per-sample spike probability, plus a correction for searching over templates, times and channels. For p ~ 1e-4 this gives A ≈ 4–5. KS's Th=8–9 is roughly twice that, which suggests the KS thresholds absorb non-Gaussian "noise", mainly multi-unit background spikes and template mismatch. This assumes KS's thresholded quantity is a whitened amplitude (z-score-like), which needs checking in `spikedetect.py` and `template_matching.py`. If it holds, Th could be reparameterized as an implied prior firing probability or an expected false-alarm rate per channel-second under an empirically measured noise distribution (for example, time-reversed or sign-flipped data). That would be a principled knob, not an arbitrary one.
- Every model-based criterion is calibrated only if its noise model is right. On Neuropixels data, "noise" is largely other neurons, so any LLR or BIC threshold will need empirical calibration (hybrid injection or the null-distribution approach above). The theory supplies the *form* of the objective; it does not set the operating point.
- Penalized-likelihood or evidence criteria (BIC, MDL, DP posterior) choose the number of units but tend to over-split heavy-tailed or drifting clusters. That is why later work moved to t-mixtures (Shoham), drift-tracking models (Calabrese & Paninski), and nonparametric unimodality tests (ISO-SPLIT).

## Q2. Which ground-truth-free quality metrics estimate per-unit false-positive and false-negative rates, and how well are they validated?

### Takeaway
False-positive (contamination) estimates from refractory-period violations are the most developed and best validated, though only in simulation. The progression runs Hill et al. 2011 → Llobet et al. 2022 → Vincent & Economo 2024 → the IBL sliding-RP metric. They are unbiased at the population level but very noisy per unit, especially at low firing rates. False-negative estimates are much weaker: amplitude-distribution truncation (Hill 2011; Allen "amplitude_cutoff"; IBL "noise cutoff") rests on a symmetry or Gaussian assumption that drift breaks. They miss spikes lost to overlaps or merges into other units. Cluster-separation metrics (isolation distance, L-ratio, d′, nearest-neighbour hit rate) rank units but are not calibrated error rates. Kilosort's own "good" label is a refractory-ratio test, not a contamination fraction.

### Cited Findings
- **Hill, Mehta & Kleinfeld 2011** (J Neurosci 31:8699–8705): propose reporting quality metrics alongside sorted units. These include (i) a false-positive estimate from refractory-period violations, assuming contaminating spikes arrive at random times, (ii) a false-negative estimate from spikes lost below the detection threshold, and (iii) cluster-overlap estimates. Implemented in UltraMegaSort2000 and cortex-lab `sortingQuality`. — [ResearchGate full-text PDF, via search](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51222913_Quality_Metrics_to_Accompany_Spike_Sorting_of_Extracellular_Signals); [UltraMegaSort2000 manual](https://neurophysics.ucsd.edu/lab/UltraMegaSort2000%20Manual.pdf); [cortex-lab/sortingQuality](https://github.com/cortex-lab/sortingQuality); [SpikeInterface isi_violation docs](https://spikeinterface.readthedocs.io/en/0.95.0/quality_metrics/isi_violations.html)
- **Allen Institute metrics** (used in Siegle et al. 2021, Nature 592:86):
  - `isi_violations` follows Hill et al. with a 1.5 ms window. It reports the *relative firing rate of contaminating neurons*, not a fraction of spikes, so 0.5 means contaminants fire at about half the rate of true spikes, and values above 1 are possible.
  - `amplitude_cutoff` estimates missing spikes by assuming the amplitude histogram is symmetric. About 15% of units hit the ceiling value of 0.5. Drift usually violates the symmetry assumption, and the documentation says it is "only weakly correlated with other measures of unit quality."
  - Default filters are amplitude_cutoff < 0.1, isi_violations < 0.5 ("still quite permissive") and presence_ratio > 0.9. The documentation suggests ≤ 0.01 amplitude cutoff for precise timing work.
  - Isolation distance depends on the number of PCs (96 dimensions by default), which makes comparisons across studies hard. d′ "in principle" estimates FP rate, but "more work is required to validate this." nn_hit_rate comes from Chung et al. 2017.
  - The page states plainly that "there is no 'ground truth' information available in these datasets" and "none of these metrics are perfect."
  — [AllenSDK quality-metrics notebook, fetched](https://allensdk.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_static/examples/nb/ecephys_quality_metrics.html); [Siegle et al. 2021, DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-03171-x)
- **Schmitzer-Torbert et al. 2005** (Neuroscience 131:1): define isolation distance and L-ratio, which measure separation in feature space. They are not calibrated FP/FN rates. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2004.09.066)
- **Harris, Quian Quiroga, Freeman & Smith 2016** (Nat Neurosci 19:1165, "Improving data quality in neuronal population recordings"): a review arguing for routine quality reporting, ground-truth datasets and shared benchmarks. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4365)
- **Neymotin et al. 2011** (J Neurosci): an information-theoretic measure of the quality of neuronal identification. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4053-11.2011)
- **Llobet, Wyngaard & Barbour 2022** (bioRxiv, Lussac): a contamination estimator from refractory violations that assumes many independent contaminants. Vincent & Economo show their model reduces to this "in the infinite-contaminant limit". SpikeInterface's `rp_violation` metric follows it. — [Lussac bioRxiv, DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.02.08.479192); relationship confirmed in [Vincent & Economo 2024, fetched](https://www.eneuro.org/content/11/8/ENEURO.0554-23.2024)
- **Vincent & Economo 2024** (eNeuro 11(8) ENEURO.0554-23.2024; bioRxiv 2023):
  - In the simplest case, FDR = ½(1 − √(1 − 2·ISIv/(τ·f_t))), where τ is the effective refractory period (refractory period minus censor period) and f_t is the cluster's total rate. This reduces to Hill 2011 (only true-positive/false-positive violations) and to Llobet 2022 (N→∞ contaminants). The final estimate averages N=1 and N=∞, roughly equivalent to N ≈ 2–3, and is capped at 0.75.
  - Positive covariance between true- and false-positive firing inflates violations, so the homogeneous model overestimates FDR. An inhomogeneous variant corrects for this.
  - For 10-minute recordings, single-cluster RMSE was 0.13 with no bias. Clusters at 1–2 Hz need "hundreds of minutes" to reach 20% CV. The same ISIv of 0.5% means 5% FDR at 20 Hz but 50% FDR at 3 Hz.
  - Population statistics are robust: the RMSE of median or mean FDR over 1,000 clusters was 0.03 or 0.02.
  - Across 12 public mouse datasets, median FDR ranged from 3.1% to 50.0% (mean 12.9 ± 13.5% SD), and mean FDR from 12.5% to 44.3%. There was no clear link to sorting method.
  - Validated against simulations only, not physical ground truth.
  — [eNeuro, fetched](https://www.eneuro.org/content/11/8/ENEURO.0554-23.2024); [BU open-access copy](https://open.bu.edu/items/6dd428a0-e331-4517-86f3-330973d12659)
- **IBL sliding refractory-period metric** (IBL spike-sorting white paper 2022, v2 2024): SpikeInterface credits IBL for `sliding_rp_violation` and `amplitude_median`. A recent PMC paper, "A flexible quality metric for electrophysiological recordings across brain regions and species", describes the sliding-RP metric. It "avoids any assumption about RP duration" and "incorporates Poisson statistics to approximately control false acceptance rates". It quantifies only the false-positive rate. Authors and venue were not confirmed because PMC returned a CAPTCHA. — [PMC13014153, snippet only](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13014153/); [SpikeInterface references](https://spikeinterface.readthedocs.io/en/stable/references.html). The IBL white paper's "good unit" criteria are, from background knowledge and unverified, sliding-RP pass, noise_cutoff < 5, and median amplitude > 50 µV. — [IBL white paper figshare, DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.19705522)
- **Bombcell** (Fabre, van Beest, Peters, Carandini & Harris 2023, Zenodo; Python version 2025): combines "five established and ten novel quality metrics" and classifies units as good, MUA, noise or non-somatic using adjustable thresholds. Later work (UnitRefine 2025) used Bombcell outputs as a baseline or proxy against human labels, because "there is no ground truth for any high-density ephys data". No ground-truth validation of Bombcell was found. — [Zenodo 8172822](https://zenodo.org/records/8172822); [GitHub](https://github.com/Julie-Fabre/bombcell); [UnitRefine bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.30.645770v1.full.pdf)
- **Kilosort's own criterion** [local code]:
  - `CCG_metrics` computes R12 as the minimum over windows of ±1…±10 ms of the ACG rate in the window divided by the baseline rate. Baseline is the larger of the far-lag (±250–500 ms) rate and the flank (10–50 ms) rates. Q12 is a Poisson-like probability for the central count.
  - A unit is "good" if R12 < `acg_threshold` (default 0.2) and Q12 < 0.2. A pair counts as cross-refractory, for splits and merges, if R12 < `ccg_threshold` (0.25) and Q12 < 0.05.
  - The code says the KS4 paper used `acg_threshold=0.1` and the default was raised to 0.2 "to better reflect common practice". R12 is returned as `est_contam_rate`, and variable names R and Q were swapped relative to earlier versions.
  — [local code: kilosort/CCG.py](file:///C:/code/github/Kilosort/kilosort/CCG.py), [kilosort/parameters.py](file:///C:/code/github/Kilosort/kilosort/parameters.py). The KS4 paper states the thresholds as ACG R12 < 0.1 with Q12 < 0.2, and CCG R12 < 0.25 with Q12 < 0.05. — [KS4 preprint, fetched](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.07.523036v1.full)
- **Barnett, Magland & Greengard 2016** (J Neurosci Methods 264:65–77): stability metrics. The sorter is rerun under perturbations that mimic natural variation, and the agreement of each unit is measured. The approach makes no noise-model assumptions and was compared with ground-truth accuracy on simulations. Stability is presented as a prerequisite for accuracy, not a measure of it. — [arXiv 1508.06936, fetched via search](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06936)

### Inferences
- **Converting the KS R12 to a contamination fraction:** in the infinite-contaminant (Llobet) picture, the ACG rate in the refractory window relative to baseline is about 1 − (1−c)² = c(2−c), for a fraction c of random contaminating spikes. So R12 < 0.2 corresponds to c ≲ 10–11%, and R12 < 0.1 (the paper value) to c ≲ 5%. With one contaminant (Hill) the mapping differs, and Vincent & Economo's correlation effects matter. KS's "est_contam_rate" is therefore a refractory ratio with a nonlinear link to FDR, not an FDR. The minimum over windows of 1–10 ms adds selection bias toward low values for sparse units.
- The FN side, which is what sensitivity mostly changes, has no well-validated ground-truth-free estimator. Amplitude-cutoff-type metrics see only losses from the detection floor, not losses from overlaps, collisions, or spikes absorbed into other templates.

- No physical ground-truth validation (juxtacellular or paired recordings) was found for amplitude-cutoff or noise-cutoff FN estimates, or for Bombcell thresholds.
- Could not verify a "Vollan et al. 2025" spike-sorting quality-metric paper. Searches returned nothing, so the reference may be mistaken or the work may be outside the indexed literature.
- Could not access the IBL white paper or the sliding-RP paper's full text to confirm thresholds and authorship.

## Q3. Can these metrics define a tuning objective (maximize units passing FP/FN bounds, or isolation-weighted yield)? What are the pitfalls?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only as a constrained-yield objective with population-level error control and independent checks. Several literature results show that naive per-unit metric optimization is gamed. Removing spikes that cause ISI violations lowers violations without lowering FDR. Per-unit ISI thresholds both admit contaminated clusters and reject clean ones. Merging units that fire in disjoint epochs passes refractory tests. Amplitude cutoff couples directly to the detection threshold being tuned. In Kilosort the refractory criterion used to label "good" units also drives splits and merges, so "number of KS-good units" is partly circular.

### Cited Findings
- Vincent & Economo argue *against* ISI violations as a per-cluster inclusion criterion, because it can keep contaminated clusters and discard clean ones. They recommend estimating *population-level* FDR to guide whether more curation is needed, and warn against removing individual spikes that cause violations, since "this lowers ISIv without meaningfully lowering FDR." — [eNeuro 2024, fetched](https://www.eneuro.org/content/11/8/ENEURO.0554-23.2024)
- Allen documentation: two neurons with similar waveforms firing in non-overlapping epochs can merge and still show low violations, which rewards over-merging. Drift breaks amplitude_cutoff and lowers PC-based metrics. Presence ratio penalizes neurons that fire selectively. — [AllenSDK notebook, fetched](https://allensdk.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_static/examples/nb/ecephys_quality_metrics.html)
- The KS4 paper says Kilosort deliberately biases clustering toward over-splitting so that curators mainly merge. Modularity reassignment "almost never made incorrect merges, and instead output some clusters oversplit." — [KS4 preprint, fetched](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.07.523036v1.full)
- In KS4 code, `ccg_threshold` controls refractory-based splits and merges, and `acg_threshold` assigns "good" labels from the same `CCG_metrics`. — [local code: kilosort/CCG.py](file:///C:/code/github/Kilosort/kilosort/CCG.py)
- Barnett et al. propose stability under perturbation as a ground-truth-free check that does not depend on the sorter's internals. — [arXiv 1508.06936](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06936)
- Benchmarking practice: KS4 scores each hybrid ground-truth unit as accuracy = 1 − FP − FN and counts it as found if the score is > 0.8. The hybrid set has 600 ground-truth units and 600 lower-amplitude multi-units from IBL recordings. Other sorters were run through SpikeInterface with "parameter adjustments ... in some cases". — [KS4 preprint, fetched](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.07.523036v1.full)

### Inferences
- **A defensible objective:** maximize N(units with estimated FDR ≤ α_FP and estimated miss fraction ≤ α_FN), or the sum over units of (1 − FDR̂ − FNR̂)₊, subject to four conditions:
  1. Population FDR is estimated with Vincent & Economo's model, using a fixed refractory window and fixed contaminant-count assumptions, not KS's R12.
  2. A minimum spike count or firing rate per unit sets estimator variance, because low-rate units cannot be certified.
  3. Units must be stable under perturbation or rerun (Barnett).
  4. The same quantities, measured on a hybrid-injection run on the same recording, calibrate the metrics. Hybrid FP/FN is the only check that cannot be gamed. The repo's existing hybrid harness fits this role.
- **Pitfalls specific to tuning Th:**
  - (a) Raising Th mechanically worsens amplitude cutoff, which measures distance to the detection floor, while lowering FP. Lowering Th does the reverse. The FN metric and the knob are tautologically linked.
  - (b) Lowering Th adds small units whose low rates make their FDR estimate unreliable. A per-unit bound then admits them by chance, the selection-under-noise effect behind Vincent & Economo's per-cluster warning.
  - (c) Low Th lets background spikes join large units' clusters. Refractory metrics underestimate this when contaminants are correlated with the true unit, as nearby neurons often are.
  - (d) Any metric that KS itself uses internally (CCG refractoriness) cannot serve as an independent objective.
  - (e) Over-splitting inflates unit counts while each fragment still looks refractory and well isolated. Yield objectives must deduplicate, for example by merging fragments by CCG or template similarity before counting, or count total spikes instead of units.
- An isolation-weighted "effective spikes" objective, Σ n_spikes × (1 − FDR̂), is less sensitive to splitting than a unit count. It still rewards absorbing multi-unit spikes into a unit until violations become detectable.

### Gaps
- No published study was found that tunes a sorter's sensitivity parameters by maximizing a ground-truth-free metric objective and then validates the result against ground truth.

## Q4. Is there a known Pareto-frontier result (better algorithms move the FP/FN frontier outward versus choosing an operating point along it), and how have papers separated the two?

### Takeaway
The distinction is explicit in classic tetrode ground-truth work. Harris et al. 2000 found a Type I/Type II trade-off in manual sorting and estimated the *best achievable* error with an optimal ellipsoidal cluster boundary ("best ellipsoidal error rate"). That defines the frontier set by the data and separates it from the operator's operating point. Modern benchmarks (KS4, SpikeForest) mostly collapse FP and FN into one accuracy score at a fixed threshold. That mixes the two: a sorter can look better by sitting at a different operating point. No modern paper was found that sweeps sorter thresholds to trace FP-versus-FN frontiers across sorters on the same ground truth.

### Cited Findings
- Harris, Henze, Csicsvari, Hirase & Buzsáki 2000 (J Neurophysiol 84:401–414), using simultaneous intracellular and tetrode recordings: "With manual spike sorting, we found a trade-off between Type I and Type II errors, with errors typically ranging from 0 to 30% depending on the amplitude and firing pattern of the cell." The lowest possible error was "estimated by a search for the best ellipsoidal cluster shape", and "human operator performance was significantly below the estimated optimum." Bursting and synchrony increased errors. — [PubMed 10899214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10899214/); [ISTA record](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/3548)
- KS4 benchmarking reports accuracy = 1 − FP − FN with a > 0.8 "found" criterion. This single scalar hides where on the FP/FN curve each sorter sits. Other sorters received only case-by-case parameter adjustment. — [KS4 preprint, fetched](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.07.523036v1.full)
- SpikeForest (Magland et al. 2020 eLife) reports accuracy, precision and recall for each sorter at default parameters, as a function of SNR. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.55167). SpikeInterface (Buccino et al. 2020 eLife) offers the comparison tools. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.61834)
- Hill et al. 2011 treat the detection-threshold FN estimate and refractory FP estimate as separate axes to report, not to combine. — [Hill 2011 PDF via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51222913_Quality_Metrics_to_Accompany_Spike_Sorting_of_Extracellular_Signals)

### Inferences
- For KS4 versus KS2.5, the right comparison is to sweep each version's sensitivity knobs (Th_learned/Th(2), and the cutoff or `acg_threshold`) on hybrid data and plot per-unit or pooled FP against FN, or precision against recall. If KS4's curve dominates, the frontier has moved. If the curves cross, the versions differ in operating point, and a fixed-threshold accuracy comparison is misleading. Precision ≈ 1 − FDR̂ and recall ≈ 1 − amplitude-cutoff or other FN estimate give an approximate ground-truth-free version of the same plot, with the caveats in Q2.
- A Harris-style "best achievable error" analogue is the error of an oracle classifier, for example LDA on ground-truth labels in template-feature space. It bounds how far a frontier can move for a given recording.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve Pedreira et al. 2012 ("How many neurons can we see with current spike sorting algorithms?", J Neurosci Methods), which shows that sorters detect only a fraction of simulated neurons, mainly limited by SNR. — [DOI, not fetched](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2012.07.010)
- Found no explicit precision–recall or ROC frontier comparison across modern sorters as thresholds vary.

## Q5. Is there work on false-discovery-rate-style control of unit inclusion or spike inclusion?

### Takeaway
Only partially. "FDR" in this literature usually means a unit's contamination fraction, which is a per-unit precision, not multiple-testing control. Vincent & Economo estimate it and recommend controlling it at the population level. The IBL sliding-RP metric is the only method found that is framed as approximately controlling a *false-acceptance rate* of units through Poisson statistics. Model-based sorters (Pillow binary pursuit, Franke Bayes-optimal matching, Wood & Black DP posteriors) give per-spike posterior probabilities that could in principle support Bayesian FDR control of spike inclusion, but no such procedure was found in practice.

### Cited Findings
- Sliding-RP metric: "incorporates Poisson statistics to approximately control false acceptance rates", and covers only the false-positive rate. — [PMC13014153 snippet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13014153/)
- Vincent & Economo: the ISI → FDR model is "framed as estimation, not control". They recommend population-level FDR estimation to guide curation. — [eNeuro 2024, fetched](https://www.eneuro.org/content/11/8/ENEURO.0554-23.2024)
- Wood & Black: a posterior over spike-train assignments, explicitly meant to carry sorting uncertainty into downstream analysis. — [Wood & Black 2008](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~fwood/papers/Wood-JNM-2008.pdf)
- Pillow et al. 2013: the posterior over binary spike trains under a Bernoulli prior, plus error diagnostics without ground truth. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0062123.html)
- KS's Q12 Poisson-like probability is a per-unit significance-style quantity, but it is thresholded (0.2 or 0.05) with no multiplicity correction. — [local code: kilosort/CCG.py](file:///C:/code/github/Kilosort/kilosort/CCG.py)

### Inferences
- A practical "FDR-style" scheme for KS tuning has three parts. (i) Compute a per-spike score: the template-match variance explained or likelihood ratio already in KS. (ii) Estimate the null distribution of that score from data with no true spikes, such as sign-inverted or time-reversed whitened data, or injected-noise residuals. (iii) Choose Th so the expected fraction of null detections among accepted spikes is ≤ q, in the spirit of Benjamini–Hochberg or local FDR. This is a proposal; no published spike-sorting validation was found.
- Unit-level FDR control across hundreds of units would require per-unit p-values for "contamination > α". These could come from Vincent & Economo's model with Poisson count noise. Low-rate units will rarely reach significance, so expect FDR-controlled yield to be dominated by high-rate units.

### Gaps
- Found no published Benjamini–Hochberg, knockoff or local-FDR procedure applied to spike or unit inclusion in spike sorting.
- Could not read the sliding-RP paper's full text to see exactly what "approximately control" means (per-unit Type I error versus population FDR).
